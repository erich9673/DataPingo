ISSUE ORDER FOR JIRA

Select and drag issues

Tick issues, then drag them to a new place or onto another epic. Each drop is checked before you let go.

First release — Latest Runs on Atlassian Atlassian Forge Jira Cloud

On this page

In Issue Order for Jira, tick the issues, then drag them where they belong. Drop between two issues to put them in that place, or onto an epic to move them under it. Several ticked issues move as one. Every drop is checked against what Jira allows before you let go, and nothing changes in Jira until you press Save changes.

Tick issues

Four ways, and they combine:

  • Click a row to tick it; click a ticked row to untick it. Anywhere on the row works except the fold arrow, the key (which opens the issue in a new tab) and the ••• button.
  • Sweep — press an unticked row, or any checkbox, and drag up or down. Every row you pass on the same level is ticked. Press a ticked row’s checkbox and sweep to untick instead. Near the top or bottom of the outline, it scrolls for you.
  • Shift-click a row to tick every row between the last one you clicked and this one.
  • From the keyboard, Space ticks the row under the cursor, and Shift with ↑ or ↓ ticks a run of rows.

The toolbar then counts them, 3 selected, with an Unselect link, and the move button reads Move 3 to…. Esc, or a click on the empty space below the last row, clears the ticks too. Ticking never changes the draft.

  • Ticks hold one level at a time: epics, child issues or sub-tasks. Tick a row on another level and the earlier ticks are cleared.
  • A ticked epic lights up its child issues with a light tint, and a single ticked issue its sub-tasks. They are what Sort sorts.
  • What you see is what moves. Ticked issues that you fold away, or that Find hides, drop out of the ticks.
  • On a touch screen, a tap ticks or unticks a row, and you drag by the grip. A swipe on a row scrolls the outline and never ticks anything.

Drag

Press anywhere on a ticked row except its checkbox, or on any row’s grip, and move. The grip is the dotted handle at the left of a row; it shows when you point at the row or tick it. The drag starts once the pointer has moved a few pixels, so a plain click on a ticked row still unticks it.

  • The grip of an unticked row drags just that issue, without ticking it.
  • A ticked row, or its grip, carries every ticked issue.

While you drag:

  • A card follows the pointer with the issue’s key and summary, or a count such as 2 issues, and where they will land: → 1.1, the number the first issue will get there, or → into IOQ-4, at the end over an epic.
  • A line shows the spot between two issues, or the row you will drop into is highlighted. The issues you carry are dimmed.
  • Folded rows open for you. Hold the pointer over a folded epic for a moment and it opens, so you can drop at an exact place inside it.
  • The outline scrolls while the pointer is near its top or bottom edge, faster the closer you go.
  • Esc cancels the drag, and so does letting go outside the app. Nothing moves.

Several issues land together, in the order they had in the outline, whichever one you grabbed, even if they were spread out or came from different epics. They stay ticked afterwards, so you can drag them again.

Between issues, or onto one

Where the pointer sits when you let go decides what happens. The rules keep an issue on its own level.

Pointer overWhen you let go
The top half of an issue on the same levelThey go just above it.
The bottom half of an issue on the same levelThey go just below it, after anything open under it.
An issue one level up: an epic when you carry child issues, or a story, task or bug when you carry sub-tasksThey move under it, to the end of its list, and it opens to show them.
An issue further down, such as a sub-task when you carry child issuesThey go just below the child issue it sits under.
The Without an epic heading, carrying child issuesThey lose their epic and go to the end of that list.

So an epic dragged over another epic goes above or below it, never inside it. With priority lanes on, a drop into another lane also changes the issues’ priority, and the card says so: · becomes High.

What Jira allows

The app checks each drop against Jira’s own rules, so a move Jira would refuse is refused before it reaches the draft, with the reason:

RuleWhat the app says
Child issues go under an epic.Child issues go under an epic.
Sub-tasks go under a standard issue, never straight under an epic.Sub-tasks go under a story, task or bug, not an epic.
Sub-tasks stay in their own space, and always have a parent.Jira keeps sub-tasks in their own space. or Sub-tasks always need a parent issue.
Without an epic takes only child issues from its own space.Only child issues go in this list. or Issues without an epic stay in their own space.
Team-managed spaces keep a parent and its children in the same space.Team-managed spaces keep parents and children in the same space.
Under epics in other spaces takes nothing in.This list shows issues whose epic is in another space. To put an issue under such an epic, use Move to… with Other spaces.
Reordering needs the Schedule issues and Edit issues permissions in that space.You can’t reorder issues in space. It needs the Schedule issues and Edit issues permissions.
A new parent needs the Edit issues permission, and placing the issue there needs Schedule issues.You can’t change key’s parent: it needs the Edit issues permission in space. or key can’t be moved: placing it needs the Schedule issues permission in space.

Company-managed spaces share epics, so a child issue may go under an epic in another company-managed space. Such an epic isn’t in this space’s outline, so use Move to… with Other spaces. Limits lists every rule in one place.

Refused drops

  • You see it before you let go. The card’s title fades, the reason shows on it in red, and no line appears. Let go anyway and the same reason appears in the status line. Nothing changes.
  • Some can go, some can’t. The card adds 1 issue can’t go there. Let go and the others move, while the status line names the ones that stayed: key stayed where it was: followed by the reason.
  • The end of a very long list. A list with more than 1,000 issues shows only its first 1,000, so its end isn’t on screen, and a drop onto its parent, which would put the issues at the end, is refused: This list has more than 1,000 issues and only the first 1,000 are shown, so its end isn’t here. Drop them next to one you can see instead. See Limits.
  • Dragging is off for a moment while Review is showing (Show everything to drag issues again. In Review you can undo changes or save.), while the Sort bar is open (Sort or cancel the sort first.), and during a save or a refresh.

What a drop looks like

The issues you moved flash briefly, and the header counts the change: Draft · 1 change. Each moved issue shows its new number in blue, its old number struck through, and a note:

You moved itIts note
Up or down in its own listMoved up from 1.6, Moved down from 1.1
To another epicFrom IOQ-1 Launch checklist
Out of Without an epicWas without an epic
Into Without an epicMoved out of IOQ-1
Out of Under epics in other spacesWas under IOT-170

Where an issue left its old list, a dashed ghost line keeps its place: IOQ-10 Step 10 Ship, moved to 4.4 in IOQ-4, 2 issues moved to IOQ-4 Small epic, or IOQ-10 Step 10 Ship, moved out of its epic. Issues that only shifted to make room renumber quietly, without a note, so the marks show what you did and nothing else.

The row ••• menu

Point at a row and click ••• at its right end:

  • Move up and Move down — one place, past the next issue you can see. Done issues that Hide done hides, and rows Find hides, are stepped over.
  • Move to top and Move to bottom — of its own list.
  • Move to… — another epic or parent; see Move to another epic.
  • Open in a new tab — the issue in Jira.

On a row ticked with others, the moves act on all the ticked issues. To move them together they must share a parent; otherwise the status line says Select issues under one parent to move them together.

The menu adapts to the row. An epic in the space’s list has no Move to…, because epics can only be reordered there. A row with an unsaved change adds Undo this change, a parent whose list you sorted adds Undo the sort, and with priority lanes on, an issue out of priority order adds Move into its priority lane. While Review is showing or the Sort bar is open, the moves are left out.

Moves from the keyboard

Dragging needs a pointer. Every move it makes is also in the ••• menu:

  1. Press Tab to reach the outline, then ↑ and ↓ to reach the issue.
  2. To move several, tick them with Space, or Shift with the arrow keys.
  3. Press Shift + F10, or the menu key, to open the row’s menu.
  4. Choose Move up, Move down, Move to top, Move to bottom or Move to… with the arrow keys, and press Enter.

Focus goes back to the outline, and a screen reader hears where the issues went, ending Not saved yet. The other keys are in About the app.

Changing your mind

  • Press the × at the end of an issue’s note, or on a ghost line, to put it back where it was.
  • Drag it again. A second move replaces the first, and moving an issue back to its old parent cancels the move.
  • Press ⌘/Ctrl + Z to undo your last change.

Review and save covers Review, Discard and what a save writes.
