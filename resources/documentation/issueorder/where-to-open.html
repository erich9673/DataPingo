ISSUE ORDER FOR JIRA

Where to open it

Two ways into the app, where each one starts, and when to use which.

First release — Latest Runs on Atlassian Atlassian Forge Jira Cloud

Documentation

Get started Installation Where to open it User guide Admin guide App technical information FAQ Help and support Release notes

On this page

Two ways in

  1. Open an issue’s ••• menu and choose Reorder child issues.
  2. Open Apps in Jira’s sidebar, choose Issue Order for Jira, then a space.

Both open the same organizer: a numbered outline in Rank order, with the same toolbar. They differ in where it starts. Each keeps its own draft, so changes made in the pop-up don’t show on the Apps page until you save them. Whichever you use, nothing changes in Jira until you press Save changes.

Both work in Jira’s software and business spaces, company-managed or team-managed. The ••• action doesn’t appear in Jira Service Management (JSM), Customer Service Management or Jira Product Discovery spaces, and the Apps page doesn’t list those spaces. This only limits where you open it from: a request from one of those spaces that sits under an epic in a software or business space still shows in that epic’s list.

From an issue: Reorder child issues

Open the issue whose child issues you want in order, open its ••• menu and choose Reorder child issues. The organizer opens in a large pop-up over the issue, already on that issue’s own list. It’s the quickest way in when you know which epic is out of order.

What the pop-up shows

Opened on…It shows
An epicIts child issues, numbered 1, 2, 3… Show offers Child issues and Sub-tasks.
A story, task or bugIts sub-tasks.
An issue on the level above epics (an initiative, on sites that have one)Its epics, with Epics, Child issues and Sub-tasks under Show.
A sub-taskNothing to order: Sub-tasks don’t have child issues. Open this on the issue above it to reorder its sub-tasks.

The header shows where you are (Shop / SHOP-1), the issue’s summary, a count such as 6 child issues, and Close. The footer holds the status line and the draft: No changes or Draft · n changes, then Discard and Save changes. An issue with nothing under it says so, for example SHOP-7 has no child issues yet.

Zoom out to the whole space

The space’s name in the breadcrumb is a link; its tooltip reads See the whole space. It shows the whole space in the pop-up, the way the Apps page does: every epic, with Without an epic at the bottom. Back to SHOP-1 takes you back to the issue you opened.

Your unsaved changes come with you both ways. Move a child issue to another epic, zoom out to put it in place there, and save everything together.

Close

Close takes you back to the issue. With unsaved changes it asks first — Leave without saving? Your n changes will be lost. — with Keep editing and Leave without saving. It can’t be pressed while a save is running.

If you saved, Jira refreshes the issue as the pop-up closes, so its Child work items or sub-task list shows the new order straight away. Where Jira can’t refresh that view, the new order shows the next time the issue opens.

From Apps: Issue Order for Jira

Issue Order for Jira under Apps opens the organizer on a full page. Use it to work on a whole space: its epics, their child issues and sub-tasks in one outline.

Choose a space

The first time, the page opens on Choose a space: the Jira spaces you can see, A to Z by name. Only software and business spaces are listed, each with its type and how it’s managed, such as Software · Company-managed or Business · Team-managed.

  • Find a space filters by any part of a space’s name, or by its whole key in any case.
  • Click a space to open it. The bar at the top then shows Space and a button with the space’s name: click it to switch to another space.
  • It remembers your space. Next time, the page opens straight on the space you used last. Your browser keeps it, for each Jira site. If that space is gone, or you can no longer see it, you choose again.
  • Switching with unsaved changes asks first — Switch to …? Your unsaved changes in … will be lost. — with Keep editing and Switch and discard. While a save or a Refresh from Jira is running, the space button does nothing; its tooltip reads Wait for the save to finish.

What a space opens on

  • A space with epics opens on its epics, numbered 1, 2, 3 in Rank order. The header counts them, then their child issues once those are loaded (8 epics · 39 child issues), and holds the draft and Save changes.
  • Show picks how deep the outline opens: Epics, then the level below, named after the space’s own work types (Stories, tasks & bugs, for example), then Sub-tasks. The first time, it shows epics only; after that your browser remembers your choice.
  • At the bottom, Without an epic lists the space’s issues that have no epic, numbered on their own. Under epics in other spaces appears only when some of the space’s issues sit under an epic in another space, and each one says which epic.
  • A space with no epic level, or no epics yet, opens on its issues instead, with Show named after its types. Business spaces often have only tasks and sub-tasks, so they open on their tasks.

Which one to use

You want to…Use
Put the child issues of the epic you’re reading in order••• → Reorder child issues on the epic
Reorder a story’s sub-tasks••• → Reorder child issues on the story
Reorder the epics themselves, or drag issues from one epic to anotherIssue Order for Jira under Apps, or the breadcrumb in the pop-up
Sort the child issues of every epic in a space at onceIssue Order for Jira under Apps
Work through several spaces, one after anotherIssue Order for Jira under Apps, switching with the space button

What both share

  • The toolbar. Find, Show (where there’s more than one level), Hide done, Numbers, Priority lanes, Sort…, Move to…, Refresh from Jira and How it works (the ?), which is worded for where you are.
  • The draft. Changes stay staged while you open and fold lists, and one Save changes writes them all.
  • A safety net. Unsaved changes are kept in your browser, separately for each space on the Apps page and each issue in the pop-up, so a closed tab doesn’t lose them. Coming back offers Restore unsaved changes.
  • Your permissions. However you came in, the app acts as you. Issues you can’t see don’t appear, and reordering needs the Schedule issues and Edit issues permissions in that space — see the Admin guide.
